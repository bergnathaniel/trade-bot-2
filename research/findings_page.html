The 51st Configuration

Fifty strategies had already failed here. This one deserved a real test — it was the only family the earlier tests were structurally incapable of finding.

Strategy diversified time-series momentum Universe 19 ETFs · 16 futures Window 2004–2026, 21.8y Basis excess of cash, net of costs

Every earlier backtest in this project had the same shape: one instrument, long-or-flat, judged against buy & hold of that same instrument.

That shape cannot find trend following. Trend following has never beaten the S&P on raw return and does not claim to — its documented value is portfolio-level: many uncorrelated markets, volatility-scaled, low correlation to equities. Testing it one asset at a time against that asset is like testing a seatbelt by checking whether it makes the car faster.

So this was a genuine gap, not a re-run. Diversified time-series momentum is also the best-evidenced systematic strategy in the literature — positive in every decade since 1880 across 67 markets in four asset classes. If anything was going to survive, it was this.

TEST 01

Does it make money on its own?

Twenty-two years, nineteen markets, volatility-targeted to 10%, costed at 5bp a side, and measured in excess of cash — the only basis on which a levered book compares honestly to buying an index.

TEST 02

Is it a genuine diversifier?

Here the answer is yes, and this part is structural rather than fitted — it falls out of holding sixteen-odd uncorrelated markets, not out of any parameter choice.

Daily correlation to the S&P is +0.016. On the 211 days SPY fell more than 2%, SPY averaged −3.20% and trend averaged +0.01%. It paid in the two crises where a diversifier has to earn its keep:

TEST 03

Did the benefit survive publication?

Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen published the 21/63/252 construction in 2012, fitted on 1985–2009 data. Everything after 2012 is therefore genuinely out-of-sample for these exact parameters — the cleanest test available, and the one that settles this.

A benefit that is large before publication and approximately zero for the fourteen years since is the signature of an effect that was arbitraged away — or was never there.

TEST 04

Has anyone actually earned it?

METHOD

Four bugs that each manufactured edge

Caught mid-build. Every one of them made a dead strategy look alive, and three would have survived a casual review. This is the part worth keeping.

  • Yahoo silently downsamples range=max

    A max request returns monthly bars with no warning or error. The first run was a monthly study wearing a daily label. Fixed by passing explicit period1/period2 epochs.

  • Continuous futures are spliced, not back-adjusted

    6J=F prints −90% then +904% on consecutive days in December 2001. The tell: a real move drags the bar's own high-low range with it, a splice does not. 54 of them across 16 markets.

  • The strategy was trading its own benchmark

    On the futures universe, SPY and IEF were loaded so the benchmarks would share a calendar — and passed straight into the traded universe. Adding an equity sleeve to a diversifier during a bull market is not a subtle error.

    futures Sharpe 0.31 → 0.27

  • No risk-free rate anywhere

    This was the big one. Without it every "Sharpe" is really just return over volatility, which massively flatters a low-volatility, levered book. The long-only variant scored 0.69 and passed the deflated-Sharpe test — until it was corrected to excess-of-cash.

    long-only Sharpe 0.69 → 0.45 · PASS → FAIL

One more, not a bug so much as a false label: the variant described as "long-only, cash-account realistic" was running at 2.05× average leverage, because the portfolio volatility scaler sat on top of the gross-exposure cap. It was never implementable as described.

STATISTICS

Pricing in how hard we looked

Ninety configurations were run — not to pick a winner, but to measure dispersion. The reported configuration ranked 76th of 90, which is the point: it is the published construction, not the best of the search.

The Deflated Sharpe Ratio asks whether a result beats what that many skill-less attempts would produce by luck alone:

Anything under 0.95 means not distinguishable from luck. Days of track record needed to clear the bar: never, at this Sharpe.

For calibration: pure Gaussian noise with a Sharpe of 0.75 scores 0.99 on its own and 0.11 against 50 trials. That gap is this project's entire history in one number.

What this settles

Diversified time-series momentum was the most credible systematic strategy never tested here. Tested properly — excess of cash, costed, splice-cleaned, deflated for multiple testing, split at its publication date, and checked against live funds — it is:

  • Not a return engine. It loses to an index fund on every measure.
  • A genuine diversifier. Correlation +0.016; it paid in the GFC and in 2022.
  • Whose diversification benefit has been ≈ zero for 14.7 years.
  • And which no live fund has converted into excess return over 15 years.

The one thing still standing post-2012 is drawdown reduction: 60/40 alone −22%, with a 20% trend sleeve −17%. That costs about 1.1%/yr of return. It is a real trade-off and an honest one — but it is a risk-management purchase, not a money-maker, and it sells in a ticker for 0.85%/yr rather than as a bot to build and babysit.

This is the 51st configuration and it fails like the other fifty — but for the first time the failure is measured against the right benchmark, with the right statistics, and corroborated by live money. That makes it a place to stop rather than another loop.
